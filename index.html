Who we are

A gear closet for the whole state

Lacrosse is the most equipment-heavy sport a kid can walk into. Before a single practice, a family is looking at a helmet, shoulder pads, arm pads, gloves, and a stick. For a lot of families that number is the reason their kid never shows up. For a lot of coaches it's the reason the program can't grow, or can't start at all.

Meanwhile the gear exists. Every spring, thousands of helmets and pads come off kids who outgrew them, and most of it goes into a garage and stays there. Pass It Forward Lacrosse is the connection between those two facts.

And it's not just outgrown gear. Anyone who's been around club lacrosse knows the cycle: a new helmet drops, a teammate shows up with the new gloves, the stick that was fine last season gets replaced because this year's head throws a little better. Club players and their families upgrade constantly, and the gear they set aside is usually a season or two old, barely worn, and better than what a lot of programs can put on a kid new. A helmet that got replaced because it wasn't the latest model is still a great helmet. Those upgrades are our supply.

We put bins at partner clubs at the end of every season. Volunteers sort and inspect everything that comes in. Helmets get checked against their NOCSAE certification and sent to a licensed reconditioner when they need it. Then complete sets go out to programs that apply, free, so a coach can put a kid on the field who otherwise wouldn't be there.

We're starting in the Puget Sound corridor, where the clubs and the high school programs are, and building the model so it can run as a local chapter in any lacrosse town on the West Coast and eventually anywhere in the country. Washington first. Then wherever there's a coach with a bin and a roster that needs it.

The gear you upgraded out of is the gear someone else is waiting on.

We serve programs, not individuals. A coach or athletic director applies for their team, receives gear in bulk, and handles fitting and distribution. No family ever has to fill out a form or explain anything to us. The coach already knows who needs it.

Pass It Forward Lacrosse is a Washington nonprofit corporation. Our application for 501(c)(3) status is in progress; donations are tax-deductible once the IRS determination is received, retroactive to our incorporation date.

What we hold ourselves to

  • Every helmet is one we'd wear. Checked, dated, recertified when it needs it, recycled when it doesn't pass. No exceptions, no favors.
  • The coach is the expert. They know who needs gear. We don't ask families to prove anything.
  • Gear stays local. What's donated in Puget Sound goes to Puget Sound programs first. Shipping is for real gaps, not for show.
  • Donors get credit, players get privacy. We name the clubs that fill bins. We never name the kid who got the helmet.
  • One number matters. How many players are on the field in donated gear. That's what we report, every season.

Who started this

From Jace and Hayden

Jace Bacon

Co-founder · Class of 2028 · Bellevue Lacrosse, Seattle Starz

Hayden Ritchie

Co-founder · Class of 2028 · Bellevue Lacrosse, Cityside

We've been playing lacrosse since before we can really remember. Youth leagues, then Bellevue Lacrosse, then club ball (Jace with Seattle Starz, Hayden with Cityside), summers full of tournaments, the whole thing. We're juniors now, and lacrosse has been the constant through all of it: the friendships, the road trips, the sore Sundays, the one more rep after practice when nobody was watching.

Somewhere along the way we noticed something that bugged us. Every season, club families like ours upgrade. A new helmet comes out, somebody gets the new gloves, and the stuff from last year goes in a bin in the garage. Perfectly good gear. Gear that still has a lot of seasons left in it. And at the same time, there are kids a few miles away who want to play and can't, because a helmet costs more than their family can put toward a sport, and whole programs that can't grow because the rack is empty.

That didn't sit right with us. We know what lacrosse has given us, and we think any kid who wants that should get the chance. So we started Pass It Forward Lacrosse: a way for the gear that's already out there to find the players who are waiting on it.

We're still in school. We still have practice. But we're the ones sorting the bins, talking to coaches, and making sure every helmet that goes out is one we'd wear ourselves. If you see us at a drive, say hi. We'd love to hear where you got your first stick.

— Jace & Hayden

How it works

Garage to field in three steps

1

Donate

Drop outgrown or unused gear in a bin at a partner club during the last weeks of the season. Helmets, pads, gloves, sticks, goalie gear, goggles. You get a receipt by email.

2

Sort & inspect

Volunteers log every item by category, size, and condition. Helmets are checked for a current NOCSAE label; those that need it go to a licensed reconditioner. Anything unsafe is recycled, never passed on.

3

Distribute

Coaches apply with roster size and what they're missing. Two weeks after each drive closes, we fill requests in a batch and deliver or ship complete sets to the program.

What we take

If it fit a lacrosse player once, it fits in the bin

We accept everything and sort it ourselves. You don't need to judge the condition; we do that. Here's what happens to each category.

Yes, bring it

  • HelmetsAny age, any condition. Checked for NOCSAE date; reconditioned or recycled as needed.
  • Shoulder, arm &
    rib pads    Straps intact, foam not crushed. We check for mold.
  • GlovesPalms intact, thumb protection present.
  • SticksHeads, shafts, or complete sticks. Men's and women's. We re-pair shafts with donated heads.
  • Goalie gearChest protectors, throat guards, goalie pants, goalie sticks. The hardest thing for a new program to afford.
  • GogglesGirls' lacrosse eyewear, ASTM-stamped, lens unscratched.
  • Jerseys, pinnies, balls, conesPrograms want these too.

Please don't

  • MouthguardsWe never reuse them. We buy new ones and include one with every helmet that goes out.
  • CleatsSizing is too specific to match well. Pass them to a teammate or a local shoe drive.
  • Non-lacrosse gearFootball, hockey, and baseball equipment goes to the sport's own programs; we can point you to one.
  • Cash in the binUse the donate button instead; it pays for helmet recertification and shipping.
LIDS

Every helmet gets the same check: shell, padding, chin strap, NOCSAE label, manufacture date. Within date and sound, it goes out. Borderline, it goes to a NOCSAE-licensed reconditioner and comes back drop-tested and stickered. Cracked, stripped, or too old, it's recycled. No helmet leaves us that a referee or athletic director could reject.

For coaches and athletic directors

Apply for gear

Two minutes. Tell us about your program and what you're short. We fill requests in a batch about two weeks after each collection drive closes, so you'll hear from us with a pickup date or a tracking number, not a form letter.

Who's eligible

Any youth or high school lacrosse program (school team, club, rec league, or a new program trying to start) where the coach or AD can say that lack of gear is keeping players off the field. No family income verification. You vouch for your program; we take your word and talk to you.

Who goes first

  1. Programs starting up or about to fold for lack of gear
  2. Title I schools and rec programs with no booster club
  3. Established programs with one specific gap (a goalie set, girls' goggles) keeping a kid benched

What happens next

  1. You get a confirmation email within a day.
  2. After the drive closes, we sort for two weeks and match requests to inventory.
  3. You get a pickup date or a tracking number, with a packing slip listing every item and its helmet dates.
  4. Your coach fits and hands out the gear. We never contact players or families.

What we'll ask after

Thirty days after delivery, one question: how many players are on the field in donated gear? That number is what gets the next program outfitted.

Not in Washington?

Apply anyway. We can't ship outside the state yet, but every out-of-area application tells us where the next chapter should be.

Questions

Things people ask

Is a used helmet safe?

A helmet with a current NOCSAE certification label, no cracks, and all its padding is what every league already requires. We don't pass on anything else. Helmets that are sound but out of date go to a reconditioner licensed by NOCSAE, who drop-tests them and applies a dated recertification label. Anything that fails is recycled.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Our 501(c)(3) application is in progress. Once the IRS issues our determination letter, donations are deductible retroactive to our incorporation date. You'll receive a receipt for every donation either way; the IRS asks the donor to value non-cash gifts, so we describe what you gave and leave the value to you.

Can an individual player or family apply?

Not directly. We work through programs so no family has to fill out a form or explain their situation. Ask your coach to apply for the team; they can request gear for however many players need it.

Do you only serve Washington?

For now. We're building the model around the Puget Sound corridor first, then adding chapters in Spokane, Oregon, and down the West Coast. If you're a coach outside Washington, apply anyway; it tells us where to go next.

What about girls' lacrosse gear?

It's its own category for us, not an afterthought. Goggles, women's heads, and goalie gear for girls' programs are among the most-requested items we get. If you have them, we need them.

Can my club host a bin?

Yes, and that's how most gear reaches us. One bin in your field house for the last three weeks of the season; we handle the rest and credit your club publicly. Email us.
